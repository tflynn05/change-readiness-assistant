# Project health check: reference

Use this when building Step 3 of the plan. The four corners mirror the common practitioner view of project health: a clear definition of success, held up by leadership and sponsorship, project management, and change management. The questions below are this skill's own; they are not a licensed assessment instrument. If the organization uses one, run that instead and report its results here.

## Scoring

Score each question 1 (not in place), 2 (partly in place), or 3 (in place). Five questions per corner, so each corner scores 5 to 15.

| Corner total | Band |
|---|---|
| 5 to 8 | High risk: this corner will undermine the others |
| 9 to 12 | Medium risk: workable, with named gaps |
| 13 to 15 | Low risk |

At planning stage, most honest scores are 1s and 2s. Score what is evidenced, not what is intended. "Not evidenced in the description" scores 1 and says so.

Score on artifacts, not on people. A named operations lead or a core team is not evidence of a project manager, a schedule, or a work breakdown; those score 2 or 3 only when the artifact itself is described. The same applies to sponsorship: a sponsor's title is evidence of authority, not of visible activity.

## Success definition

1. Can the business benefit be stated in one sentence that an impacted employee would understand?
2. Are there measurable objectives with units, and does each have an owner?
3. Is it stated what share of the benefit depends on people adopting and using the change, as opposed to the technology working?
4. Are adoption and usage targets written down, separate from the delivery milestones?
5. Do the sponsor and the unit leaders describe success the same way?

## Leadership and sponsorship

1. Is there one primary sponsor with authority over the people, budget, and systems in scope?
2. Can the sponsor explain the change, the reason, and the benefit without notes?
3. Is the sponsor visibly active now, resolving issues and speaking to employees, rather than only at kickoff?
4. Is there a coalition of unit leaders who have each committed to the same definition of success?
5. Has the change's priority against other initiatives been set and communicated?

## Project management

1. Is there an assigned project manager and a schedule with milestones?
2. Is the scope defined, including which units go live when?
3. Are the resources identified and secured?
4. Is the nature of the change documented: who is impacted and how, by group?
5. Does the project plan contain the change work as tasks with owners and dates, or is it a separate track?

## Change management

1. Has anyone assessed impact by group (what changes in each group's work)?
2. Has the change risk been assessed: size of the change against readiness of the organization?
3. Is there a change strategy sized to that risk, approved by the sponsor?
4. Has the sponsor coalition been assessed for strength, and gaps addressed?
5. Is there a named owner for the change after the project team leaves, and a plan to sustain it?

## Reading the results

- **All four corners high risk** is common at planning stage and is not a reason to despair; it is the reason the plan exists. Say so.
- **Success weakest**: build the definition of success first. Nothing else can be sized.
- **Sponsorship weakest**: the plan's first deliverable is a sponsor conversation with a specific ask, not a communications calendar.
- **Project management weakest**: the change plan has nothing to attach to; get the schedule and the impact-by-group documented.
- **Change management weakest, others stronger**: the project is well run and will be well executed, and people will revert anyway. Front-load the diagnostics (Steps 1 and 2) and get adoption targets into the project plan.

Report the three lowest-scoring questions overall; they are usually the first three actions.
