# Ground truth: what the practitioner actually found

This is what the skill's output is graded against. It comes from two assessments I completed on the rollout as coursework for my Prosci Change Practitioner certification, after my time on the program had ended, and from what I observed leading operations for it. The assessments reflect the program as I understood it at that point; they are one practitioner's read, not an official project record. The blind run did not see any of it.

The assessments are Prosci instruments. Only the scores and my own characterization of the results are recorded here, not the instrument content.

## Project health check (PCT assessment)

Four corners scored out of 30. All four landed in the high-risk band.

| Corner | Score | What drove it, in my words |
|---|---|---|
| Success | 15 | The benefit and objectives were defined in general terms, but there were no units of measure, no named benefit owners, no evaluation of how much of the benefit depended on people, and no definition of success ready to communicate. The sponsor coalition was not aligned on one. |
| Leadership and sponsorship | 14 | The strongest single item: a primary sponsor with the necessary authority was in place. The gaps were in the sponsor activities the model looks for next: sustained visibility through the lifecycle, a coalition of senior leaders across units, direct communication with employees, and an explicit priority call against competing work. These are structural conditions of the program, not a judgment of any individual. |
| Project management | 16 | Highest corner, still high risk. The nature of the change, including who is impacted and how, was well defined. Objectives and scope were partly defined. There was no project manager assigned for the full lifecycle, no complete schedule, no work breakdown, and resources were not yet secured. The lowest-scoring item was the degree to which change management was integrated into project planning. |
| Change management | 14 | Some structured activity was underway (plans being built, effectiveness partly monitored), but the diagnostics underneath it were missing: no impact assessment, no change risk assessment, no adoption and usage objectives, no coalition assessment, no sponsor-approved strategy, and no preparation to sustain the change after the project. |

## Readiness assessment (ADKAR kick-off assessment, Marketing Channel Managers)

| Awareness | Desire | Knowledge | Ability | Reinforcement | Barrier point |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 1 | **Desire** |

Desire was the first element at or below 3. Knowledge, Ability, and Reinforcement scored lower but build on Desire, so the conclusion was that more announcements and earlier training were the wrong spend, and the plan had to start with the actions that resolve Desire: managers acknowledging job impacts, leaders modeling the change, addressing low trust from past changes, involving channel managers in design, and an honest "what's in it for me."

Only Marketing Channel Managers were formally assessed. The other four impacted groups (campaign operations, marketing analysts, LOB marketing leads, compliance approvers) were my working list, drafted during certification pre-work and marked for confirmation, not assessed.

## What I observed

- The technology worked. Getting people to change how they built, approved, and measured campaigns was the hard part.
- The resistance had a specific shape: skepticism about the AI itself, discomfort with reduced control, and uncertainty about what a person's role became once the model was making the decisions.
- Stakeholders stalled at Desire and Ability far more than at Awareness. People knew the change was coming.
- Across lines of business, sponsor consistency was the single biggest predictor of adoption. Where unit-level sponsorship was visible and active, adoption moved; where it was not, it lagged.
- People-side work was sequenced after technical delivery rather than alongside it. That is the pattern I would change first on the next rollout, and the skill is built to start the diagnosis at planning stage for that reason.
- For the certification presentation, the audience I chose was the primary sponsor and the ask was commitment to the three sponsor roles (active and visible participation, building a coalition, communicating directly with employees), because both assessments pointed at sponsorship as the critical gap.
